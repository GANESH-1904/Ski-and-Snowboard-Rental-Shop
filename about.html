On this mountain since 2012

Built by technicians who were tired of bad rental gear.

VÉRTEX ALPS started in a single workshop at the base station, run by two former World Cup service techs. They had spent years watching guests leave shops with wet liners, blunt edges and bindings set by guesswork, and decided a rental could be held to the same standard as a race room.

Our Timeline

How the depot grew

Fourteen seasons, one mountain, and a workshop that kept outgrowing its room.

2012

One room behind the ticket office

Forty pairs of skis, a hand file and a wax iron. Bookings came in by phone the night before.

2015

The bootfitting lab opens

A pressure plate and a shell stretcher arrive, and boot returns drop by two thirds in a single season.

2018

Robotic stone grinder installed

Every ski and board starts getting the same finish, measured rather than judged by feel.

2021

First heated locker wall

Ninety pods beside the gondola remove the morning counter queue entirely.

2024

Mid-mountain and north peak depots

Gear swaps become possible without riding back to the valley floor.

2026

450 pods and a fluorine-free workshop

The full fleet runs on plant-based wax, and the locker wall is powered by the roof array above it.

Our Standards

Four things we refuse to compromise on

The standard applied to every ski tune, every board edge and every locker pod before it goes out.

1

No queue in the morning

A digital key opens a heated pod at the gondola, so nobody spends first light standing at a counter.

2

Machine-finished edges

Ceramic discs give a smooth, repeatable finish that hand files cannot match on a fleet this size.

3

Boots measured, not guessed

Instep height, calf shape and arch length decide the shell, rather than the number on your street shoe.

4

Nothing toxic in the meltwater

Plant-based waxes only. What comes off the bases in spring ends up in the stream below the village.

The tuning bay

Same finish on the hundredth pair as on the first

Hand filing leaves small burrs that catch unpredictably on hard snow. The stone grinder gives every base the same structure and every edge the same angle, night after night.

  • Half a degree of base bevel, so turns start smoothly instead of grabbing
  • Two degrees on the side edge for grip on steep, icy pitches
  • A cross structure ground into the base to clear water film on warm days
  • Every pair inspected under a light bar before it goes into a locker

Looking after the catchment

The snow we ski on becomes the water below

Fluorinated waxes persist in snowpack and wash into the valley when it melts. We stopped using them in 2019, before the ban, and have not lost a second of glide worth arguing about.

  • Plant-based, biodegradable wax across the entire fleet
  • Wood cores sourced from certified alpine forestry
  • Retired skis rebuilt into trail fencing and avalanche signage
  • Locker heating runs on the solar array above the depot roof

Inside VÉRTEX ALPS

Inside the depot

A clean look at the equipment, fitting and service work that happens before every day on the mountain.

01 · Rental floor

Ready for the first lift

Skis, snowboards and accessories are organized so the team can size, prepare and hand over equipment without slowing down the morning rush.

02 · Mountain service

Gear moves with the mountain

Our depot workflow connects the rental floor with the slopes, making equipment changes and last-minute adjustments simple during the day.

03 · Workshop

Tuned before it leaves

Every pair is serviced at the bench, then lined up by size before it returns to the fleet, so what leaves the workshop is consistent and ready to ride.

04 · Precision fitting

The final adjustment

Boots and equipment receive their final fit and adjustment before collection, so riders leave the depot ready for the snow.
